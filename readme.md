# UAV Identification: Advantages of Radar-Based UAV Detection and Classification Based on Mixed Quantum Neural Networks at Low Signal-to-Noise Ratio [with Python Code]

This study investigates the performance of hybrid quantum neural networks (HQNN) and their classical counterparts, convolutional neural networks (CNN), in the detection and classification of drones using radar. Specifically, we employ a complex radar time series model originating from electromagnetic theory, the Martin-Mulgrew model, which is used to simulate radar echoes from objects with rotating blades such as drones. The findings reveal that CNN outperforms HQNN in terms of detection and classification when the Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR) is high. However, under low SNR conditions, which are the most critical for real-world applications, HQNN demonstrates superior performance compared to its architecture-similar CNN counterparts.
Introduction: Machine learning has successfully been applied to various problems. Quantum machine learning (QML) is an emerging field that integrates principles of quantum computing with classical machine learning techniques, aiming to enhance the efficiency and capabilities of classical machine learning algorithms through the use of quantum phenomena [1][2][3].
Hybrid quantum neural networks (HQNNs) are a significant area of research within QML, combining classical deep learning architectures with variational quantum circuits (VQCs) among other quantum algorithms to process data [4][5][6].
The application of HQNNs has been explored across multiple domains, including drug reaction prediction [7], dementia detection [8], multiclass tasks [9][10], remote sensing image classification [11], network attack detection [12], facial image gender recognition [13], and financial and MNIST data classification [14][15].
The widespread application of drones in various fields has prompted the need for reliable methods for effective detection and classification. A primary reason is the statistical model complexity of radar echoes, which often cannot be solved analytically in many cases. Previous studies have explored applying machine learning and deep learning to this problem [16][17][18][19].
In this paper, we apply the HQNN for drone detection and classification problems. Previous studies have used CNNs to detect and identify drones [20][19]. This study extends the analysis to HQNN and compares its performance with architecture-similar CNNs. Specifically, as in previous work, we use time series data of drone reflection radar signals simulated based on the Martin-Margulis (MM) model, obtained through Short Time Fourier Transform (STFT) of radar microDoppler features. We investigate these data in X-band radar simulation scenarios, examining binary classification between drones and noise for radar detection, and comparing the performance of the HQNN model with classical CNNs on multiclassification tasks. The findings show that using HQNN has a significant advantage in low signal-to-noise ratio environments (the most critical scenario in real applications). Compared to architecture-similar CNNs, HQNN performs better at lower signal-to-noise ratios. The research strongly suggests that HQNN should be explored for application in radar time series analysis.

Conclusion: This study investigates the application of hybrid quantum neural networks (HQNNs) in the task of drone detection and classification, comparing their performance to that of classical convolutional neural networks (CNNs) with similar architectures. We observed that even at extremely low signal-to-noise ratios such as -5, -10, -15, and -20, both classical models and hybrid models demonstrated significant detection accuracy. At a signal-to-noise ratio of -20 dB, the F1 score for drone detection using CNN was 0.745±0.0273922, while HQNN performed better, achieving an F1 score of 0.843±0.0434856. At a signal-to-noise ratio of -5 dB, the F1 score for drone classification using CNN was 0.654±0.009712, whereas HQNN achieved a higher F1 score of 0.696±0.002887 on the same task. Overall, HQNN outperformed its counterpart CNN, especially under lower signal-to-noise ratio conditions, highlighting its importance in researching environments with very low signal-to-noise ratios.
In future work, transfer learning can be utilized: the model can be retrained with real data that may capture subtle differences or changes not perfectly captured by synthetic data. Pre-trained models enable us to achieve good performance even when limited real data is available, which is particularly beneficial given the scarcity of real data. Future work can also explore other possible radar parameters and different neural network architectures, including the use of quantum convolution layers, to develop more robust solutions.
This work is preliminary. For example, even when the neural network model is trained on appropriately low signal-to-noise ratio data, significant detection accuracy can be achieved even when the signal-to-noise ratio is below -20 dB. More complex CNN architectures might outperform HQNN. Nevertheless, it is worth noting that HQNN has clearly outperformed in this important practical problem, thus there is a need to further explore the application of quantum-inspired algorithms in this crucial real-world issue.
